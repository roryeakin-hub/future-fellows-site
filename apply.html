Thank you for applying.

We've received your application and it's now in our review queue. Real people read every one of these — nothing here is automated past this confirmation.

What happens next

  1. We review every application against our intake criteria across Skills, Agency, and Professionalism. This takes real time — we don't send instant decisions.
  2. If there's a fit, we'll reach out directly by email to schedule a short conversation.
  3. Qualified applicants join our bench — a pool we draw from when a specific, paid project with an employer partner becomes available for your track.

Questions in the meantime? Reach Rory directly at info@joinfellows.org.

A note on where we are

Future Fellows is early. We're building the employer side of this alongside the fellow side, in parallel, in public. That means the bench you're joining today may take time to convert into a matched project — we'd rather tell you that plainly than oversell where we are.

What that also means: if you're one of our first fellows, you're not joining something already proven. You're helping prove it.

While you wait

Five pieces we think are worth your time, regardless of what happens with your application.

  • What You'll Wish You'd Known
    Paul Graham
    A case against the standard "figure out your passion" advice — and for optionality instead, especially before you're forced to specialize.
  • Advice for Ambitious 19 Year Olds
    Sam Altman
    A tactical framework for choosing between school, a company, or a startup — built from patterns across many people who asked him this exact question.
  • Nobel Laureate Claudia Goldin on "Greedy Work" and the Wage Gap
    Claudia Goldin — 2023 Nobel Laureate in Economics, on Freakonomics Radio
    Why some employers pay disproportionately more for near-total availability — and what that means for which industries and roles to target early.
  • On Passion and Its Discontents
    Cal Newport
    The tightest version of his "career capital" argument: build rare, valuable skills first — passion and autonomy follow, not the other way around.
  • When to Quit: A Simple Framework for Life's Toughest Decisions
    Annie Duke, on Big Think
    Former pro poker player turned decision scientist on why "never quit" is bad advice — and a concrete framework for knowing when to walk away from a path.
